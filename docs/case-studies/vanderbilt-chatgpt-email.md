# Vanderbilt University ChatGPT Email Controversy - February 2023

## Incident Overview

**Institution**: Vanderbilt University Peabody College (Tennessee)  
**Date**: February 16, 2023  
**Failure Mode**: None (human misuse of an LLM writing tool, not an agent failure). Staff asked ChatGPT to draft the text and it did; the failure was the decision to use it for a condolence message.  
**Impact**: Public criticism, apology, two deans temporarily stepped back during a review  
**Scope**: Email to Peabody College students

## What Happened

Following the mass shooting at Michigan State University on February 13, 2023, which killed three students and injured five others, Vanderbilt University's Peabody College Office of Equity, Diversity and Inclusion (EDI) sent an email to students about the shooting.

At the bottom of the email was a line crediting ChatGPT.

**The Attribution Line**: "Paraphrase from OpenAI's ChatGPT AI language model, personal communication, February 15, 2023"

## The Email Content

### The Message
The email read in part:

> "The recent Michigan shootings are a tragic reminder of the importance of taking care of each other, particularly in the context of creating inclusive environments. As members of the Peabody campus community, we must reflect on the impact of such an event and take steps to ensure that we are doing our best to create a safe and inclusive environment for all."

The Vanderbilt Hustler noted that the email referred to multiple "recent Michigan shootings," though only one had occurred.

### The Problem
The email was signed by two EDI administrators and a graduate student; reports name Associate Dean Nicole Joseph among the signers. The disclosure line showed students that the message had been drafted with ChatGPT.

## Student and Community Response

**Laith Kayat**, a Vanderbilt senior from Michigan whose younger sister attends Michigan State, called the use of ChatGPT "disgusting."

**Kayat's Statement**: "There is a sick and twisted irony to making a computer write your message about community and togetherness..."

## Administrative Response

### The Apology

**Nicole Joseph**, Associate Dean for EDI, sent a follow-up email on February 17, 2023, calling the use of ChatGPT "poor judgment."

**Joseph's Apology**: "While we believe in the message of inclusivity expressed in the email, using ChatGPT to generate communications on behalf of our community in a time of sorrow and in response to a tragedy contradicts the values that characterize Peabody College."

### Dean's Response

In a February 18 email, **Dean Camilla Benbow** said the university's administrators, including herself, were unaware of the email before it was sent. The Vanderbilt Hustler reported that its crafting and distribution did not follow Peabody's typical review process for communications.

**Dean Benbow's Statements**: 
- She said she was "personally saddened" by the loss of life at Michigan State.
- "I am also deeply troubled that a communication from my administration so missed the crucial need for personal connection and empathy during a time of tragedy."

### Administrative Consequences

Benbow's office said it would review what led to the email being sent. **Associate Dean Joseph and Assistant Dean Hasina Mohyuddin temporarily stepped back from their EDI roles** during the review.

## Root Cause Analysis

The AI tool did what it was asked: it produced a condolence message. The failure was human:

1. **Inappropriate Tool Choice**: Staff used an LLM to draft a message responding to a tragedy, which students and the dean saw as lacking personal connection.
2. **Review Process Not Followed**: According to the dean, the email did not go through Peabody's normal review before it was sent.

## Lessons Learned

1. **Human Connection**: Messages responding to a tragedy were judged by students and the dean on whether they showed personal connection; an LLM-drafted message failed that test.
2. **Review Processes Matter**: The email skipped the college's normal review.
3. **Disclosure Is Not a Defense**: Crediting ChatGPT did not make its use acceptable to recipients.
